# Groundhog Vault demo narration (scene, seconds)

[hook · 9.66s]
A treasury agent puts thirty percent into a pool. The pool depegs. Eighteen thousand dollars, gone. Then the session ends, and the agent forgets it ever happened.

[product · 3.85s]
Groundhog Vault is a treasury agent that remembers the cost of failure.

[setup · 8.89s]
It is a controlled experiment. Two agents get the same capital, the same decision logic, and the same market. Only one of them can read Sibyl memory.

[life1 · 7.04s]
Life one. Both allocate thirty percent to MoonPool. Both lose eighteen thousand dollars. Identical by design.

[destroy · 10.54s]
Then both runtimes are destroyed. The only survivor is Sibyl: an incident entity, an append-only event, and a risk policy keyed by the risk signature, not the protocol name.

[life2 · 13.54s]
Life two. SunPool has a new name, but the same signature. A fresh Groundhog session recalls the policy and caps exposure at five percent. Amnesiac repeats thirty. Memory preserved twelve thousand three hundred dollars.

[walkthrough · 6.29s]
That is the whole claim. Persist the loss. Recall it by signature in a fresh session. Change the decision.

[treasury · 12.54s]
It works on a treasury's own history too. Record a real incident, then evaluate a differently named proposal in a brand new session. The recommendation is five percent, and it cites the policy and the incident behind it.

[base · 9.91s]
The user signs that recommendation as a decision receipt on Base Sepolia, linking the allocation to hashes of its policy and source incident. The agent never holds the wallet.

[close · 3.41s]
Groundhog Vault. Do not pay for the same lesson twice.
