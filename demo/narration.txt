Groundhog Vault is a treasury agent that remembers the cost of failure.

This controlled experiment gives two agents the same capital, decision logic, market inputs, and deterministic outcomes. In Life One, both allocate thirty percent to MoonPool and lose eighteen thousand dollars.

Both runtimes are then destroyed. The Sibyl database is the only state that survives.

In Life Two, SunPool has a different name but the same underlying risk signature. A fresh Groundhog session recalls the prior incident and caps exposure at five percent. The memory-disabled control repeats thirty percent. Persistent memory preserves twelve thousand three hundred dollars.

The evidence view links the original incident to the stored policy and the decision it changed.

The treasury workflow accepts a user-submitted loss. A differently named proposal is then evaluated by a new agent session using the persisted Sibyl policy.

That recommendation can be signed by the user and recorded as a decision receipt on Base Sepolia, linking the allocation to hashes of its policy and source incident. Groundhog never holds the treasury wallet or executes unrestricted transactions.
